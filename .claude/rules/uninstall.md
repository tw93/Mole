# Uninstall rules (Mole CLI (Windows))

Moved verbatim from the Safety Rules of `AGENTS.md` on 2026-11-10; the cross-cutting safety contract stays there and applies here too.

- Uninstall sizes come from the registry `EstimatedSize` (KB); a value over 10 GB is checked against the measured `InstallLocation` and the measured size wins when it is at least 10x smaller (#1717). Duplicate uninstall entries merge, but never across two different install folders, and selection is keyed by registry key or package name, not display name.
- `mo uninstall` runs each uninstaller once, as registered (MSI, Inno and NSIS flags only when detected), and sends the leftover install folder to Recycle Bin only when `Test-MoleLeftoverOwnership` accepts it after the uninstall key is gone. A drive root, the profile or its standard folders, `ProgramData`, `Program Files` and `Windows` are never leftovers, and neither is a Steam library: its root (a folder with a `steamapps` child or `libraryfolder.vdf`, the Steam client's folder included), `steamapps` or anything in it outside one game's `steamapps\common\<Game>`. Registered install folders containing game-library or emulator content stay out of leftover removal. Check public folder names, GOG/Steam file markers and the `.egstore` folder of an Epic Games Launcher game at the root and immediate children with complete bounded visibility; unknown or redirected children retain the folder. Keep registered directory identity and display-size measurement independent of content retention, so protected content cannot collapse distinct registrations during merging. The content predicate runs in preview and the final ownership guard, while ordinary installs and single Steam games remain eligible. Store packages Windows depends on (Store, App Installer, Windows Security, Game Bar and other `Microsoft.Windows.*` packages) are not offered. App-cache v7 invalidates cached Store rows that lacked readable manifest evidence. Re-read the exact Store package identity and both metadata/sparse-manifest eligibility at preview and the final removal boundary; unknown package inventory or unreadable/incomplete manifest evidence retains the target at discovery, preview and removal. The required Package/Properties node must be readable; a present AllowExternalContent flag must explicitly be false or 0. The exact Proton Pass 1.37.0 / Proton AG / Update.exe `--uninstall -s` registration uses its matching registered `--uninstall` command; do not retry uninstallers or generalize this exception. Locked by `tests/Uninstall.Tests.ps1`.
- The uninstall confirmation and dry-run preview identify charge-limit maker apps by exact Store identity or Lenovo Vantage Service plus a Lenovo publisher. The notice says the limit survives uninstall and can be changed beforehand; it never changes eligibility, selection or execution. Locked by `tests/Uninstall.Tests.ps1`.
- Right before launch `mo uninstall` rereads the uninstall entry and rebuilds the command; an entry gone after an earlier row's uninstaller ran counts as done, an entry gone before anything ran is left behind (not done), and a changed or unreadable command is logged as `FAILED` and not run. The command exits 1 when any app fails, keeps its uninstall entry or is skipped as an ineligible Store package, and `mole.ps1` passes that on. `mo clean` keeps exit 0: locked files skipped on a recurring run are normal, not failures. Locked by `tests/Uninstall.Tests.ps1` and `tests/Router.Tests.ps1`.
- Registry uninstallers retain `Start-Process -Wait` descendant tracking in a cancellable runspace with a fixed 30-minute ceiling. This is a conservative wait bound, not an idle detector or user setting. Timeout stops the batch, preserves leftovers and leaves the vendor running; registration disappearance alone never proves completion. Unreadable registration remains installed. Cleanup exceptions cannot replace timeout/cancellation and advance the batch. `tests/UninstallWait.Tests.ps1` uses real disposable parent/child processes to check normal exit, descendant waiting and survival on timeout/cancellation; `tests/Uninstall.Tests.ps1` locks the batch stop after registration disappears.
- Uninstall leftovers use the dedicated shell `ITransferSource.RecycleItem` operation through `Remove-SafeItem -Recycle`. Never fall back to permanent deletion when recycling fails. Preview and the post-sizing guard independently verify complete installed-app ownership; preserve same, parent or child shared locations and unknown inventories. Recheck root identity and protected descendants after sizing, keep dry-run read-only, and do not count recycled bytes as freed space. Locked by `tests/Recycle.Tests.ps1`.
